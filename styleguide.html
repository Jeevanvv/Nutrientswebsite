Style guide

Every token and component used on this site, in one place. Part 2 of the brief is the source of truth; anything below that departs from it is marked.

Colour

Palette and where each colour is allowed

Dark Green

#2E4213

Headings, authority surfaces, footers, corporate pages

Leaf Green

#8BC53D

Accents, confirmation states, data highlights, eyebrow rules

Primary Green

#8BC53D

Primary CTAs only — its scarcity is what makes it work

Light Green

#A9D967

Highlights, calendars, child and membership pages

Soft Green Tint

#F5FAEC

Page ground, call-out boxes, warmth

Leaf Ink

#55771A

Text-weight leaf. Added because #8BC53D fails contrast as text

Two rules that matter more than the rest

Primary Green at full strength appears on primary calls to action and nowhere else. The moment it decorates something, every CTA on the site gets weaker.

Leaf Green at full strength fails contrast as text on light grounds. It stays on rules, dark-ground eyebrows and data accents; text-weight leaf uses Leaf Ink.

Accessibility, as built

The floor, applied sitewide

RequirementHow it is met here
Text contrastBody and UI text pass 4.5:1. The one deviation — white on Accent Orange — was resolved in favour of contrast and is flagged in the stylesheet
Type size17px body sitewide, 17.5px on Community, Child, Special Needs, Women’s Health and Preventive Care
KeyboardEvery control reachable and operable, with a visible focus ring. Tabs support arrow keys
Motionprefers-reduced-motion respected everywhere. Special Needs ships with motion off by default, not as a fallback
Colour independenceNo state is signalled by colour alone — pressed chips, open accordions and comparison tables all carry text or an icon
Tap targets48px minimum on every interactive element
ImagesSlots carry descriptive alt text drawn from the shot list. No text baked into images
FormsVisible labels, never placeholder-only. Errors and confirmations announced through a live region
No JavaScriptContent is fully readable. Reveal animations are opt-in via a js class on the html element
