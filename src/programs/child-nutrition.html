What this is

Building a child's relationship with food before it needs repairing

The Child Nutrition Program supports growth, immunity, cognitive development and eating behaviour in children — working with the parent and the household kitchen rather than issuing restrictions to a child.

Eating patterns established in childhood persist for decades. So does the emotional relationship with food. A child pressured, bribed or shamed at the dinner table carries that into adulthood — and arrives, twenty years later, as a client who has failed five diets. This program is the earliest, cheapest and most effective intervention Nutrinest offers.

How it runs

Delivered online through parent consultations with age-appropriate child involvement. Includes growth monitoring, deficiency correction, feeding behaviour coaching and practical lunchbox and family-meal planning. Megha's Child Nutrition specialisation anchors this program.

What this program sets out to do
  • Support age-appropriate growth and development with adequate, balanced nutrition.
  • Resolve fussy eating and mealtime conflict without coercion.
  • Correct common childhood deficiencies — iron, vitamin D, protein, B12.
  • Address childhood obesity and early insulin resistance without introducing food shame.
  • Equip parents to make decisions confidently, long after the program ends.

Who is it for

Who this is for

Parents, usually. The child is the client; the household is the intervention.

Parents of fussy or selective eaters

Mealtimes have become a daily conflict. They need behavioural strategy, not a nutrient chart.

Parents of underweight children or those with poor growth

Concerned about growth percentiles and need structured, calorie-dense, realistic feeding plans.

Parents of children with excess weight

Need weight-neutral, shame-free strategy that protects the child's self-image while changing the household environment.

Parents of children with diagnosed deficiencies

Iron, vitamin D, B12 or calcium deficiency requiring food-first correction alongside medical supervision.

Working parents

Limited time, dependent on tiffin and outsourced cooking. Need systems, not idealism.

Parents of teenagers

Navigating puberty, growth spurts, sports demands, body image and increasing food autonomy.

Families wanting to prevent rather than treat

No current problem; want to establish good patterns early.

Problems we solve

What parents are usually dealing with

  • Mealtimes have become negotiation, bribery or conflict.
  • Growth or weight concerns get a supplement prescription and no feeding strategy.
  • Children are put on adult-style diets, planting the seeds of lifelong disordered eating.
  • Lunchboxes come home untouched and nobody knows why.
  • Packaged 'health' foods marketed to children are assumed to be healthy.
  • Screen-time eating has replaced any awareness of hunger and fullness.
  • Parents receive contradictory advice from family, the internet and the paediatrician.
  • Teenagers are absorbing body-image pressure with no nutrition literacy to counter it.

If three of these are happening at your table, we can help.

Age pathways

What we work on changes as the child does

Same principles, different pressures. Pick the stage you are in.

What we work on

Establishing variety before preferences harden, iron and vitamin D adequacy, self-feeding, and ending the pressure at mealtimes that creates refusal in the first place.

What parents ask

“She eats four foods.” Selective eating at this age is common and usually responds to structure, exposure and calm — not to bribery or hiding vegetables.

What we never do

Restrict energy. A toddler's job is to grow. We change what is offered and how, not how much a child is allowed.

What we work on

Lunchbox planning that survives a real school day, breakfast that holds until the interval, and deficiency correction where growth or bloodwork suggests it.

What parents ask

“The box comes back full.” Usually a packing problem rather than a child problem — texture, temperature, time and the twenty minutes they actually get to eat.

What we never do

Single the child out. Household-level changes work; a separate plate for one child does not.

What we work on

Growth-spurt nutrition, calcium and iron at the point of highest demand, screen-time eating, and the first conversations about what food marketing is doing to them.

What parents ask

“Is this weight a problem?” Sometimes it is a growth phase and sometimes it is not — percentile tracking over time answers it better than a single reading.

What we never do

Comment on a child's body in front of them. Weight is discussed with parents; food is discussed with the child.

What we work on

Puberty and growth demand, sports fuelling, iron in menstruating teens, skin and energy, and independence — because they are already making most of their own food decisions.

What parents ask

“He only eats outside.” We plan around what they will realistically choose, then improve it, rather than writing a plan they will ignore.

What we never do

Encourage restriction or body monitoring. We watch for disordered patterns and raise them early, with the parent.

How it works

The Nutrinest approach

Ten stages, in order. Nothing here is optional and nothing here is decoration.

01

Assessment

Growth history and percentiles, birth and feeding history, medical history, deficiency reports, current intake, screen habits and mealtime dynamics.

02

Consultation

Parent-led consultation. The child joins where age-appropriate, so they are participants rather than subjects.

03

Nutrition Planning

Age-appropriate planning built around the family's actual meals — the child eats what the household eats, adjusted.

04

Lifestyle Coaching

Sleep, screen time, play and activity are addressed alongside food, because they drive appetite regulation.

05

Behaviour Change

Feeding-responsibility model: the parent decides what and when; the child decides how much. No bribing, no coercion, no clean-plate rules.

06

Habit Building

One change at a time — a new food introduced repeatedly without pressure, a fixed mealtime, a screen-free table.

07

Follow-up

Scheduled reviews tracking growth, intake variety, mealtime conflict and parental confidence.

08

Monitoring

Growth percentile tracking, deficiency marker retesting and food-variety logs.

09

Education

Parents learn label reading, portioning by age, and how to evaluate the marketing aimed at their child.

10

Long-term Support

Transition planning through each developmental stage — toddler, school age, puberty, adolescence.

We never put a child on a diet

Not once, at any age, for any reading on any chart. Children are growing, and restriction at this stage predicts a worse relationship with food later, not a better one. When weight is a concern, we change the household food environment, the structure of meals and the activity pattern — and we do it without the child ever being made the project.

What we do and do not do

Nutrinest does not diagnose medical conditions and does not alter prescribed medication. We work alongside your paediatrician, never in place of them. We do not put children on weight-loss diets, and we will tell you plainly if something we see needs a doctor rather than a nutritionist.
