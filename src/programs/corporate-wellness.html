What this is

An annual wellness partnership, not a single health camp

The Corporate Wellness Program partners with organisations to build healthier employees, healthier food environments and healthier workplace cultures through science-backed nutrition, preventive healthcare and continuous engagement.

Most corporate wellness spend produces an event, a photograph and no measurable change. Nutrinest engagements are structured as programs with a baseline, an intervention, a tracking mechanism and an outcome report — which is the only form in which wellness survives a budget review.

How it runs

Delivered onsite, online or hybrid. Engagement models range from project-based camps to monthly retainers to annual wellness partnerships. Every engagement follows the Nutrinest framework: Assess → Analyze → Educate → Personalize → Implement → Track → Improve → Sustain.

What this program sets out to do
  • Establish a baseline health profile of the workforce before designing any intervention.
  • Improve the workplace food environment at its source — the cafeteria.
  • Deliver measurable change in employee health markers over a defined period.
  • Give HR and leadership defensible data on wellness spend.
  • Sustain engagement across the year instead of spiking once and decaying.

Problems we solve

You ran a camp last year. What changed?

  • Wellness spend produces an annual camp and no measurable outcome.
  • The cafeteria quietly undoes every awareness session run that year.
  • Employees receive health reports with no interpretation and no follow-up.
  • Awareness sessions are generic and unrelated to the actual risk profile of the workforce.
  • Engagement spikes on the day of the event and disappears within a week.
  • Leadership is offered the same program as everyone else, and doesn't attend.
  • No baseline exists, so no improvement can ever be demonstrated.
  • Shift workers and manufacturing floors are excluded from programs designed for desk employees.

If three of these describe your last wellness initiative, the model is the problem, not the budget.

The differentiator

We audit the cafeteria too. Most programs do not.

Your employees eat one to two meals a day inside your building. A wellness program that ignores what is served there is a program that argues with itself every lunchtime.

  • Current menu review across the full rotation, not a sample day
  • Cooking method assessment — oil, salt, reheating, frying load
  • Portion analysis at the counter, as served
  • Nutrition analysis of the standing menu
  • Menu optimisation within your existing cost ceiling
  • Healthy recipe development with your kitchen team
  • Vendor consultation, because the recommendations are only useful if the people cooking can implement them
  • Implementation support and a re-audit
