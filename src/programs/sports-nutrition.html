What this is

Fuelling performance, recovery and longevity — built by a national-level athlete

The Sports Nutrition Program builds fuelling, recovery and body composition strategy for athletes and serious recreational sportspeople — from junior competitors to endurance amateurs and gym-based strength athletes.

Athletes are the most heavily marketed-to and least well-advised group in nutrition. Most are either under-fuelling in pursuit of leanness or over-supplementing in pursuit of shortcuts. Megha's background as a national-level swimmer means this program is written by someone who has trained under load, not just read about it.

How it runs

Delivered online with periodised planning aligned to the athlete's training calendar. Includes competition-day protocols, travel strategy and coordination with the athlete's coach where the athlete wishes.

What this program sets out to do
  • Match energy and macronutrient intake to actual training load, not a generic template.
  • Time nutrition around training, competition and recovery windows.
  • Improve body composition without compromising performance or health.
  • Prevent low energy availability, injury and the burnout it produces.
  • Give honest, evidence-based supplement guidance rather than sales.

Periodisation

Your intake should move with your training block

Same athlete, five different nutritional demands across a season. Eating the same way through all of them is the most common mistake in this sport.

Build

Peak

Competition day

A protocol you have already rehearsed in training

Nothing on this timeline should be new on the day. All of it is trialled in the build phase first.

T–3hMain pre-event meal

Familiar carbohydrate, low fibre, low fat, moderate protein. Portion rehearsed in training, not guessed.

T–1hTop-up

Small, easily digested carbohydrate. Fluid to a planned volume based on your sweat rate, not thirst.

Warm-upFinal fluid and, where relevant, electrolyte

Set by the conditions and your measured sweat sodium, not by whatever the team is drinking.

EventIn-competition fuelling

Only for events long enough to need it. Gram targets per hour, with a practised delivery method and a backup.

0–60 min afterRecovery window

Carbohydrate and protein to a target, plus fluid to replace measured loss. Matters most when you compete again within 24 hours.

Supplement position

We do not sell supplements, and we will tell you which ones do nothing

No affiliate links, no stockist arrangement, no house brand. That is why the advice below is worth reading.

Contamination is a real risk in this market, and a positive test does not care that the label did not mention the compound. Where a supplement is warranted, we point you to batch-tested products and explain how to check.

EvidenceExamplesOur position
SupportedCreatine monohydrate, caffeine, whey or a protein source where intake falls short, vitamin D where deficient, iron where deficientUse where it fits your sport, dose and testing status
SituationalBeta-alanine, sodium bicarbonate, nitrate, electrolyte products, carbohydrate gelsWorth trialling for specific event types. Rehearse in training first
MarketingFat burners, detox products, most testosterone boosters, most greens powders, BCAAs alongside adequate proteinNo. Money better spent on food, sleep and coaching

What we do and do not do
