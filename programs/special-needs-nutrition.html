Special Needs & Neurodiverse Nutrition

Nutrition support that starts with the child in front of us, not the diagnosis.

Our position, before anything else

Nutrinest does not treat, cure or claim to reverse autism or any developmental condition

What nutrition can do is real and worth having: correct deficiencies, ease constipation and reflux, widen a narrow diet safely, support sleep and energy, and reduce the distress around eating for the whole family. What it cannot do is change a diagnosis. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. We work alongside your paediatrician, therapist and dietitian team, and we will say clearly when something is outside our scope.

What this is

Nutrition support that starts with the child in front of us, not the diagnosis

This program provides nutrition support for autistic children and adults, children with ADHD, sensory processing differences, Down syndrome, cerebral palsy and other developmental conditions — focusing on nutritional adequacy, sensory-aware feeding and gut comfort.

Families in this space are targeted relentlessly by unproven, expensive and sometimes harmful dietary protocols promising to treat or cure a condition. They deserve a nutrition professional who is honest about what nutrition can and cannot do — who can improve a child's adequacy, comfort and mealtime experience without claiming to change who the child is.

How it runs

Delivered online with an extended intake, a slower pace, and close coordination with the family's existing therapy team. What this program sets out to do
  • Achieve nutritional adequacy despite a highly restricted accepted-food range.
  • Expand food variety through sensory-informed, pressure-free strategies.
  • Address gut symptoms that frequently accompany neurodevelopmental conditions.
  • Support families with realistic strategies rather than aspirational protocols.
  • Provide an honest, evidence-based position on the diets marketed to these families.

Who is it for

Who this is for

Families, carers and the professionals working with them.

Parents of autistic children

Often managing extreme food selectivity, sensory aversion and significant nutritional gaps.

Parents of children with ADHD

Navigating appetite suppression from stimulant medication, irregular eating and energy volatility.

Families managing sensory processing differences

Food refusal driven by texture, smell, temperature or colour rather than preference.

Parents of children with Down syndrome

Growth, thyroid, constipation and weight considerations specific to the condition.

Families managing cerebral palsy

Feeding difficulty, texture modification, energy density and hydration needs.

Adults who are neurodiverse

Manage their own nutrition around sensory needs, executive function and routine dependence.

Therapy teams and special schools

Occupational therapists and educators seeking nutrition input as part of a coordinated plan.

Problems we solve

What families usually arrive with

  • The accepted food range has narrowed to a handful of items, and adequacy is genuinely at risk.
  • Families are sold restrictive protocols with strong claims and weak evidence.
  • Feeding difficulty is treated as behaviour when it is sensory.
  • Chronic constipation or gut discomfort goes unaddressed for years.
  • Stimulant medication suppresses appetite and no one has adjusted the eating window.
  • Generic nutrition advice assumes a flexibility the child does not have.
  • Parents are exhausted, blamed, and out of strategies.
  • Practitioners over-promise, and families pay for it emotionally and financially.

If any of these are familiar, you are in the right place — and you are not doing this badly.

How it works

The Nutrinest approach

Ten stages, in order. Nothing here is optional and nothing here is decoration.

01

Assessment

Extended intake: diagnosis, therapies in place, sensory profile, accepted food list, textures tolerated, gut symptoms, medication, growth and deficiency reports.

02

Consultation

Longer, slower first consultation with the parent or carer. No rushing, no judgement about how narrow the food list is.

03

Nutrition Planning

Adequacy first: meet needs within the currently accepted range, then expand carefully. We never remove a safe food without a replacement.

04

Lifestyle Coaching

Routine, mealtime environment, seating, lighting, noise and timing — all treated as nutrition variables.

05

Behaviour Change

Sensory-informed exposure: food chaining and graded exposure, at the child's pace, never coerced.

06

Habit Building

One micro-change at a time. Progress is measured in months, and that is stated openly at the outset.

07

Follow-up

Regular reviews focused on adequacy, gut comfort and accepted-food count.

08

Monitoring

Growth tracking, deficiency markers, bowel regularity and food-variety logs.

09

Education

Parents are given an honest evidence position on the popular protocols so they can evaluate future claims independently.

10

Long-term Support

Ongoing support across developmental transitions and into adult independence.

For carers

The pace is set by the child, not by the program

Intake takes longer here. Sessions are longer and quieter. Progress is measured in one new accepted food, one calmer mealtime, one less distressing morning — and those are real outcomes, not consolation prizes.

  • Extended first consultation, with time for the full history
  • Sensory and texture profiling before any food change is suggested
  • One change at a time, held until it is stable
  • Written summaries after each session, because you should not have to remember it all
  • Coordination with the therapy team where you want it
  • Permission to pause. Families in this program need that more than they need a schedule

An evidence note

Elimination diets are widely marketed for neurodiverse children and the evidence for most of them is weak. Some carry real risk of narrowing an already narrow diet. Where a trial is genuinely warranted, we run it properly — structured, time-limited, monitored, with a reintroduction plan — and we stop it when it does not help.

What we do and do not do

Nutrinest does not diagnose, treat or claim to reverse autism or any developmental condition, and does not alter prescribed medication. We work alongside your paediatrician and therapy team. Nutrition support here is about deficiency, digestion, sleep, dietary variety and mealtime distress — nothing more, and nothing less.
