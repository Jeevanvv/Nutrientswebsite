NGO & Community Health Program

Nutrition science applied where the need is greatest and the budget is smallest.

What this is

Nutrition science applied where the need is greatest and the budget is smallest

This program partners with NGOs, CSR teams, foundations and grassroots health organisations to deliver nutrition intervention, training and program design for underserved populations — addressing malnutrition, anaemia and lifestyle disease in low-resource settings.

The gap in community health nutrition is rarely knowledge — it is translation. Guidelines exist; what's missing is someone who can convert them into something an anganwadi worker can deliver on a real budget, in a real kitchen, with the foods that are actually available. That translation is the work.

How it runs

Delivered as project-based consulting or ongoing partnership: needs assessment, intervention design, frontline worker training, material development, implementation support and impact measurement. Pricing is structured to be accessible to the sector.

What this program sets out to do
  • Design nutrition interventions that work within real budget and supply constraints.
  • Train frontline workers so capability stays after the engagement ends.
  • Address the double burden — undernutrition and rising obesity in the same population.
  • Produce measurement frameworks that satisfy funders and reflect reality.
  • Ensure interventions respect local food culture rather than replacing it.

Problems we solve

Why community nutrition programs stall

  • Interventions designed for a budget the program does not have.
  • Nutrition education delivered in language and formats the community cannot use.
  • Frontline workers given targets without training.
  • Anaemia treated as a supplement problem rather than a food-system problem.
  • The double burden ignored — undernutrition and obesity coexisting and treated as one issue.
  • Impact measured in sessions delivered rather than outcomes changed.
  • Local food culture dismissed in favour of imported dietary models.
  • Programs ending when the grant ends, leaving no retained capability.

If three of these describe a program you have run, the design is the problem, not the community.

Capability transfer

The program should still be running after we leave

We train the people already embedded in the community rather than building a dependency on visiting experts.

Train the frontline

ASHA workers, anganwadi staff, community volunteers and field teams — trained in the material they will actually deliver.

Leave the materials

Session decks, flipcharts, counselling scripts and screening formats, in the working language, yours to keep and reuse.

Build local supervision

A supervision structure and refresher rhythm so quality does not decay after month three.

Hand over measurement

Indicators, formats and a reporting cadence your team can run without us.

How it works

The Nutrinest approach

Ten stages, in order. Nothing here is optional and nothing here is decoration.

01

Assessment

Baseline needs assessment: demographic profile, prevalence data, food access, local availability, cooking practice and existing infrastructure.

02

Consultation

Working with the organisation's field team — they know the community; we bring the nutrition science.

03

Nutrition Planning

Intervention design built around locally available, affordable, culturally accepted foods.

04

Lifestyle Coaching

Hygiene, water, feeding practice and maternal nutrition addressed alongside food.

05

Behaviour Change

Community-led approaches. Peer educators outperform external experts in sustained behaviour change, consistently.

06

Habit Building

One achievable practice at a time — an iron-rich food weekly, complementary feeding at six months.

07

Follow-up

Field review cycles with the delivery team, adjusting to what actually happened.

08

Monitoring

Anthropometric tracking, anaemia prevalence, dietary diversity scores and program reach.

09

Education

Frontline worker training and train-the-trainer, so capability remains after we leave.

10

Long-term Support

Sustainability planning and handover from day one, not as an afterthought at grant close.

Case snapshots

Anonymised engagements

District maternal nutrition program

Context: high anaemia prevalence, limited counselling capacity. Intervention: worker training, counselling scripts, screening formats. Outcome: reported at endline.

Urban community kitchen partner

Context: meals served daily with no nutritional standard. Intervention: menu analysis, cost-neutral reformulation, cook training. Outcome: reported at re-audit.

School feeding NGO

Context: attendance-linked feeding with unmeasured impact. Intervention: menu standards, growth monitoring, teacher training. Outcome: reported annually.

Photography note: document dignity, never deprivation. No photographs of malnourished children, and never compose Nutrinest as rescuer.
